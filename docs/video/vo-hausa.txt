Hausa Voice-Over Script — CHW Companion
========================================

For the Fiverr seller: this is the full Hausa VO. Six numbered lines, total
runtime ~80 seconds including the pauses between lines (3–4 s gaps so the
editor can match them to the storyboard). Documentary tone — warm, calm,
female, age 25–45. Not announcer, not commercial. Take a breath between
sentences; do not rush.

> **For the user (you, before posting the gig):** I am not a native Hausa
> speaker. The orthography below is academic standard Hausa (Latin
> transliteration of Boko script). Phrases like names of facilities and
> medical terms should be reviewed by the seller before recording — they are
> the kind of thing where a non-native draft can sound stiff or slightly off.
> Ask them to flag and suggest natural alternatives; the meaning is what
> matters, not literal word-for-word fidelity.

================================================================================
LINE 1 — 0:08–0:18 (10s)
================================================================================

  Sunana Aisha. Ni ce kaɗai mai aikin lafiya a ƙauye nan.

  Phonetic guide (for non-Hausa speakers):
  "soo-NA-na aa-EE-sha. nee chay ka-DEYE my eye-KEEN la-FEE-ya a kow-yay nahn."

  English meaning:
  "My name is Aisha. I am the only health worker in this village."

================================================================================
LINE 2 — 0:18–0:30 (12s)
================================================================================

  Yau zan ga Fatima. Ƙafafunta sun kumbura.

  Phonetic:
  "yow zahn gaa fa-TEE-ma. ka-fah-FOON-tah soon koom-BOO-rah."

  English meaning:
  "Today I'll see Fatima. Her ankles are swollen."

================================================================================
LINE 3 — 0:30–0:45 (15s)
================================================================================

  Babu hanyar yanar gizo a nan. Likita yana da nisan sa'o'i huɗu.

  Phonetic:
  "BAH-boo hah-N'YAR yah-NAR GEE-zo ah nahn. lee-KEE-ta ya-na da NEE-sahn sah-OH-ee HOO-doo."

  English meaning:
  "There is no internet here. The doctor is four hours away."

  Note for seller: "hanyar yanar gizo" = "way of the world-web" = internet/signal.
  An equally natural rendering is "Babu sigina" (no signal) — your choice.

================================================================================
LINE 4 — 0:45–1:10 (25s)
================================================================================

  Yanzu wayata ta na saurara. Tana gani. Tana tunani. Duk ba tare da intanet ba.

  Phonetic:
  "YAHN-zoo wah-YAH-tah tah nah SOW-rah-rah. tah-NAH GAH-nee. tah-NAH TOO-nah-nee. dook bah TAH-ray dah in-tah-NET bah."

  English meaning:
  "Now my phone listens. It sees. It thinks. All without the internet."

  Note: this is the hero line. Pause briefly after each sentence — the editor
  wants beat-on-beat alignment with the on-screen demo steps.

================================================================================
LINE 5 — 1:10–1:25 (15s)
================================================================================

  Tana kira ga kayan aiki da suka dace. Tana rubuta takarda da ta dace.
  Tana magana da Hausa.

  Phonetic:
  "tah-NAH KEE-ra ga KAH-yan AH-ee-key da soo-kah DAH-chey. tah-NAH roo-BOO-tah tah-KAR-dah da tah DAH-chey. tah-NAH ma-GAH-nah da HOW-sa."

  English meaning:
  "It calls the right tools. It writes the right form. It speaks back — in
  my language."

  Note: "kayan aiki" literally "tools of work" — closest natural Hausa phrase
  for "tools" in the LLM/function-call sense. If the seller prefers a more
  technical word, e.g. "ayyuka" (functions), that's acceptable.

================================================================================
LINE 6 — (closing alternative line in case English VO is delayed) — optional
================================================================================

  Yanzu ba dole sai na yi tsammani.

  Phonetic:
  "YAHN-zoo bah DOH-lay sigh nah yee TSAH-mah-nee."

  English meaning:
  "Now I don't have to guess."
