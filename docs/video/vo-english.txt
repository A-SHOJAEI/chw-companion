English Voice-Over Script — CHW Companion
==========================================

Tone: calm, low-key, BBC-documentary. Mid-30s female, neutral mid-Atlantic
or West African English. Not US-broadcast. Not breathy. The video is not
selling — it's explaining.

The English VO covers 1:25–2:52 (87 seconds, ~190 words). The 0:00–1:25
opening is in Hausa with burned-in English subs; see `vo-hausa.txt`.

================================================================================
SEGMENT 1 — 1:25–1:38 (13s) — TECH CREDIBILITY
================================================================================

  This is Gemma 4. Open weights. One hundred and forty languages. Audio,
  vision, and text — in one forward pass. Running on the device.

  Word count: 24
  Notes: "Gemma 4" — say it like "gem-uh four." Do NOT say "Gemini." Pause
  briefly after each clause; let the architecture diagram breathe.

================================================================================
SEGMENT 2 — 1:38–1:55 (17s) — THE MOAT
================================================================================

  A cloud API can't help Aisha. There's no signal in her village.
  Nigerian law says patient data can't leave the country. And the API
  fees alone would exceed her training budget.

  Word count: 35
  Notes: This is the strongest 17 seconds of the video. Pace it carefully.
  The "and" at the start of the fourth sentence is doing real work — pause
  before it so the audience hears the third reason as a separate punch.

================================================================================
SEGMENT 3 — 1:55–2:10 (15s) — CLOSURE FOR FATIMA
================================================================================

  When Aisha gets back to town, the visit syncs. The hospital in Kano
  knows Fatima is coming.

  Word count: 19
  Notes: First mention of Kano (KAH-no). Drop slightly on "is coming" so
  it feels resolved, not breathless.

================================================================================
SEGMENT 4 — 2:10–2:25 (15s) — SCALE
================================================================================

  Two hundred thousand community health workers in Africa. One-point-
  eight billion people more than two hours from a hospital.

  Word count: 24
  Notes: Numbers are the load-bearing words. Slow down on "two hundred
  thousand" and on "one-point-eight billion." Music builds here — the VO
  should still cut through.

================================================================================
SEGMENT 5 — 2:25–2:40 (15s) — UNIVERSALITY
================================================================================

  Gemma 4 speaks one hundred and forty languages. Hausa today. Swahili,
  Quechua, Bambara — next week.

  Word count: 21
  Notes: The four language names should each land — give them micro-
  pauses. "Quechua" is "KEH-chuh-wah." "Bambara" is "bahm-BAH-rah."

================================================================================
SEGMENT 6 — 2:40–2:52 (12s) — HEART
================================================================================

  Aisha told us something we won't forget. "Now," she said, "I don't
  have to guess."

  Word count: 17
  Notes: The shift from third person to direct quote is the emotional
  pivot. After "told us something we won't forget," pause for two beats
  before "Now." The quote should sound spoken, not announced — closer to
  a sigh than a declaration.

================================================================================
CLOSING — 2:52–3:00 (8s)
================================================================================

  [No VO. Single piano note. On-screen card reads: "CHW Companion. A
  midwife in every pocket. Built with Gemma. Apache 2.0."]

================================================================================
